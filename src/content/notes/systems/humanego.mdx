Training manipulation policies on robot teleoperation requires hundreds of hardware-specific demonstrations <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§1" />. Human egocentric video is much faster to collect—a person wearing Project Aria smart glasses <Cite id="project-aria-paper" /> can record a task in $30\text{–}40\text{ s}$ anywhere—but human hands and robot grippers differ in both visual appearance and 3D kinematics <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§1; App. D.2" />. HumanEgo bridges both sides of this embodiment gap without any robot demonstrations or internet-scale pretraining, reaching $92.5\%$ average real-world success from $30\text{ min}$ of human video per task ($75.0\%$ from $15\text{ min}$) and transferring zero-shot across Trossen, Franka, and UR10 arms <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="Abstract; §4.1; §4.3" />.

<ModelOverview model="humanego" />

Four ideas make zero-shot transfer work from minutes of video <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§1; §3" />:
- **Visual and kinematic embodiment bridge:** each frame has the human arm inpainted out, a virtual gripper and object keypoints rendered in its place, and the thumb–index pair mapped to an $\mathrm{SE}(3)$ gripper pose via MCP joints <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.2; §3.3; App. E.3" />.
- **Interaction-Centric Tokens (ICT):** every hand and object becomes a $29\text{-D}$ entity token combining its pose in a frozen episode reference frame with the relative poses of both hands in that entity's local frame, which are exact viewpoint-invariant <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.3, Eq. 1" />.

Two further choices handle learning from scarce trajectories and deployment <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.4; §3.5" />:
- **Flow-matching decoder with dense auxiliary objectives:** a 6-layer [[transformer-block]] decoder generates $50$-step bimanual [[action-chunking|action chunks]] via [[flow-matching]], while object-motion, 2D-trace, and latent-consistency heads amplify supervision <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.4, Eqs. 2–3; App. F.1" />.
- **Frontend swap at deployment:** the trained policy weights stay untouched on the robot; only the upstream perception modules swap from multi-view SLAM triangulation to static RGB-D depth back-projection and arm forward kinematics at $10\text{ Hz}$ <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.5; App. G.1" />.

## Visual and kinematic embodiment bridge

Passing raw human video directly to a policy fails on a robot ($7.5\%$ success on Water Flowers) because the visual stream shows human skin and fingers while the kinematic labels come from a 21-joint hand skeleton recorded by a moving head-mounted camera <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§4.4, Fig. 9; App. E" />. HumanEgo removes the human body from both streams before the policy ever sees them <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.2; §3.3" />.

On the visual branch, a phase detector drops walking, the $\sim 1\text{ s}$ pre-episode head sweep, and transition buffers, keeping only active manipulation frames ($v_{\text{stop}} < 0.03\text{ m/s}$, $w_{\text{stop}} < 0.15\text{ rad/s}$ for $\ge 15$ frames) <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="App. E.1; App. E.2, Tab. 1" />. SAM2 segments the arm, LaMa inpaints the background, and a virtual gripper plus object keypoints are rendered onto the $240 \times 320$ frame <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.2; App. F.1, Tab. 1" />.

On the hand branch, defining a gripper frame from thumb and index fingertips degenerates during a pinch because the tips converge and collapse the jaw axis <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.3; App. E.3" />. HumanEgo uses the fingertip midpoint for position $\mathbf{p}_{\text{ee}} = \tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{p}_{\text{thumb tip}} + \mathbf{p}_{\text{index tip}})$, and builds $R_{\text{ee}} = [\mathbf{x}_{\text{ee}} \mid \mathbf{y}_{\text{ee}} \mid \mathbf{z}_{\text{ee}}] \in \mathrm{SO}(3)$ from the wrist $\mathbf{p}_w$ and thumb/index MCP knuckles ($\mathbf{p}_{\text{tMCP}}, \mathbf{p}_{\text{iMCP}}$), which never collapse <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="App. E.3, Eqs. 5–6" />:

$$
\mathbf{x}_{\text{ee}} = \widehat{\mathbf{p}_{\text{iMCP}} - \mathbf{p}_{\text{tMCP}}}, \qquad \mathbf{y}_{\text{ee}} = \widehat{\tilde{\mathbf{y}} - (\tilde{\mathbf{y}}^\top \mathbf{x}_{\text{ee}})\mathbf{x}_{\text{ee}}}, \qquad \mathbf{z}_{\text{ee}} = \mathbf{x}_{\text{ee}} \times \mathbf{y}_{\text{ee}}
$$

where $\tilde{\mathbf{y}} = \tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{p}_{\text{tMCP}} + \mathbf{p}_{\text{iMCP}}) - \mathbf{p}_w$ and $g = \mathrm{clip}((\|\mathbf{p}_{\text{thumb tip}} - \mathbf{p}_{\text{index tip}}\| - d_{\min}) / (d_{\max} - d_{\min}), 0, 1)$ <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="App. E.3, Eqs. 6–7" />.

On the object branch, Aria Gen1 lacks a depth sensor, so each demo starts with a $\sim 1\text{ s}$ ($\sim 30$-frame) head sweep on a static scene <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="App. E.1, Tab. 1" />. Grounding DINO and SAM2 segment each object, CoTracker3 tracks $N$ contour keypoints across the sweep, SVD triangulation lifts them to 3D via the Aria MPS SLAM pose $T_{\text{SLAM}}$ to give $\mathbf{p}_{\text{obj}}$, and Orient-Anything V2 estimates $R_{\text{obj}}$ <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.3; App. E.1, Eq. 4" />. From grasp onset $t_0$, **kinematic latching** locks the occluded object pose to the hand <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.3" />:

$$
T_{\text{obj}}^t = T_{\text{hand}}^t \cdot (T_{\text{hand}}^{t_0})^{-1}\, T_{\text{obj}}^{t_0}
$$

## Interaction-Centric Tokens (ICT)

Unstructured 3D point clouds in camera coordinates leave hand–object relationships implicit and tie every coordinate to the camera viewpoint <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.3; §4.1" />. HumanEgo treats each hand and object as an entity $k \in \{1, \dots, N_e\}$ and encodes its spatial state into an Interaction-Centric Token $\mathrm{ICT}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{29}$ <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.3, Eq. 1" />.

Each $\mathrm{SE}(3)$ transform is flattened into $9$ numbers by concatenating a $3\text{-D}$ normalized translation with a continuous $6\text{-D}$ rotation representation, giving the $29\text{-D}$ bimanual token ($20\text{-D}$ in single-hand tasks, which omit the second hand's $9\text{-D}$ block) <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.3, Eq. 1" /><Cite id="humanego-repo" where="utils/ict.py; training/FlowMatchingModel.py" />:

$$
\mathrm{ICT}_k = \Bigl[\underbrace{\tau}_{1} \;\Big\|\; \underbrace{{}^{\mathrm{REF}}T_E}_{9} \;\Big\|\; \underbrace{{}^ET_{LH}}_{9} \;\Big\|\; \underbrace{{}^ET_{RH}}_{9} \;\Big\|\; \underbrace{g}_{1}\Bigr] \in \mathbb{R}^{29}
$$

Here $\tau$ identifies the entity type ($1$ left hand, $2$ right hand, $3$ anchor object, $4$ other object), and ${}^{\mathrm{REF}}T_E$ is entity $k$'s pose in the fixed episode reference frame $\mathrm{REF}$ (the camera pose frozen at the first manipulation frame during human recording, or the static camera frame on the robot) <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.3, Eq. 1; §3.5" /><Cite id="humanego-repo" where="utils/ict.py" />.

The relative blocks ${}^ET_{LH} = T_E^{-1} T_{LH}$ and ${}^ET_{RH} = T_E^{-1} T_{RH}$ express the left and right hands in entity $k$'s local frame $E$, while $g$ stores the grasp state (aperture in $[0, 1]$ for hands; $-1$ sentinel for objects) <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.3, Eq. 1" /><Cite id="humanego-repo" where="utils/ict.py" />. Because $T_E^{-1} T_H$ multiplies out any world-to-camera extrinsic, the $18$ hand–entity numbers in every object token are **exact viewpoint-invariant** <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.3" />.

## Flow-matching policy and dense auxiliary objectives

Minutes of human video contain only a few dozen trajectories ($45$ demonstrations per task for $30\text{ min}$), which makes a policy trained only on hand actions prone to overfitting <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§1; §3.4; Tab. 1" />. HumanEgo pairs a 6-layer [[flow-matching]] transformer decoder with a learnable region-attention spotlight and three dense auxiliary heads that predict 3D object motion, 2D visual traces, and future latent ICT states <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.4, Eqs. 2–3; App. F.1, Eq. 8" />.

<ModelDiagram
  model="humanego"
  caption="One of the 6 transformer decoder blocks in HumanEgo: the 50 action-chunk tokens attend to each other via bidirectional self-attention and read the 308 RGB + ICT context tokens via cross-attention biased by a Gaussian spotlight around the active anchor object."
/>

Given $\mathbf{x}_t = (1 - t)\mathbf{x}_0 + t\mathbf{x}_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times D_a}$ at $t \sim \mathcal{U}(0, 1)$ with $\mathbf{x}_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$, the decoder predicts $\mathbf{v}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t, s_t)$ toward $\mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_0$ <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.4; App. F.1" />. With error $\Delta(\cdot) = \mathbf{v}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t, s_t) - (\mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_0)$, the loss weights position ($w_p = 5$), rotation ($w_r = 1$), and grasp ($w_g = 10$) alongside three auxiliary losses <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.4, Eqs. 2–3; App. F.1, Tab. 1" />:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{FM}} = \mathbb{E}_{t, \mathbf{x}_0, \mathbf{x}_1}\!\Bigl[w_p \|\Delta \mathbf{p}\|^2 + w_r \|\Delta \mathbf{r}\|^2 + w_g \|\Delta g\|^2\Bigr], \qquad \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{FM}} + \lambda_{\text{OM}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{OM}} + \lambda_{\text{2D}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{2D}} + \lambda_{\text{LC}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{LC}}
$$

During cross-attention, **region attention** focuses the $300$ visual patch tokens on the active anchor's 2D projection $(u_0, v_0)$ via a Gaussian spotlight with learnable scale $\sigma$ <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="App. F.1, Eq. 8" />:

$$
w(u, v) = \exp\!\left(-\frac{(u - u_0)^2 + (v - v_0)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)
$$

The three auxiliary heads supervise complementary spaces without extra labels <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.4; App. F.1" />:
1. **Object motion ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{OM}}$):** 9-D future 6-DoF object trajectory over $K = 50$, weighted by $0.5(w_p, w_r)$ <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.4; App. F.1, Tab. 1" />.
2. **2D trace ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{2D}}$):** $K \times 3 \times 2$ normalized 2D coordinates of anchor keypoints ($\lambda_{\text{2D}} = 20$) <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.4; App. F.1, Tab. 1" />.
3. **Latent consistency ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{LC}}$):** ICT hand tokens $K$ steps ahead with masked MSE ($\lambda_{\text{LC}} \in [0.1, 1.0]$) <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.4; App. F.1, Tab. 1" /><Cite id="humanego-repo" where="training/FlowMatchingModel.py" />.

<Callout type="note" title="Paper vs. released code on auxiliary heads">
In `training/FlowMatchingModel.py`, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{OM}}$ (`use_aux_obj_dynamics`) appends the $9\text{-D}$ object pose to each action-chunk token ($D_a = 20 \to 29$ bimanual, $10 \to 19$ single-hand), while `head_2d_trace` replaces the paper's deconvolution stack with a 2-layer Mish MLP (`384 → 256 → K × targets × 2`) on pooled context tokens alongside a `head_done` termination head ($w_{\text{done}} = 0.2$) <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="App. F.1" /><Cite id="humanego-repo" where="training/FlowMatchingModel.py" />. Because auxiliary losses coincide with the baseline once data reaches $\ge 18\text{ min}$ (Fig. 5), the released 30-min configs set `use_aux_*: False` by default <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§4.2, Fig. 5" /><Cite id="humanego-serve-bread-config" where="use_aux_obj_dynamics, use_aux_visual_foresight, use_aux_temporal_contrastive" />.
</Callout>

At $15\text{ min}$ of human data on Water Flowers, each auxiliary objective improves over the $50.0\%$ baseline, and all three combined add $+25.0$ percentage points ($75.0\%$) <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§4.4, Fig. 10" />.

## Zero-shot robot deployment and results

When moving from human training video to a physical robot, nothing inside the policy checkpoint changes: only the upstream perception modules that build the RGB image and ICT tokens are swapped <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.5; App. G.1" />.

<Stepper title="From human Aria recording to closed-loop 10 Hz robot execution">
  <Step title="Reference frame alignment (REF)">
    During human training, the head-mounted Aria camera moves, so the pipeline freezes the camera pose at the first manipulation frame (`cam0`) as the episode reference frame $\mathrm{REF}$; across $45$ demonstrations, natural head variation covers a distribution of viewpoints. At deployment, the static top-down RealSense D405 camera frame serves directly as $\mathrm{REF}$, so any reasonable camera placement falls inside the training distribution <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.5; App. G.1" />.
  </Step>
  <Step title="One-shot RGB-D object pose initialization">
    Instead of a pre-episode head sweep and multi-view triangulation, the robot captures static RGB-D frames at episode start. Grounding DINO and SAM2 segment each prompted object once, depth back-projection computes its 3D centroid $\mathbf{p}_{\text{obj}}$, and Orient-Anything V2 estimates $R_{\text{obj}}$ <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.5; App. G.1" />.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Per-cycle observation assembly (10 Hz)">
    Every $100\text{ ms}$ on a single RTX 4090, the robot frontend segments and inpaints the physical robot arms, renders the virtual gripper and object keypoints, reads arm forward kinematics for $T_{\text{ee}}$ and $g$, and propagates grasped objects via kinematic latching to assemble the $29\text{-D}$ ICT tokens <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.5; App. G.1" />.
  </Step>
  <Step title="20-step Euler ODE rollout and smoothed control">
    Starting from $\mathbf{x}_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$, the policy integrates $\mathbf{x}_{t + \Delta t} \leftarrow \mathbf{x}_t + \mathbf{v}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t, s_t)\Delta t$ over $20$ Euler steps ($\Delta t = 1/20$) to emit a $K = 50$-step [[action-chunking|action chunk]]. The controller subsamples with stride $2$ ($5\text{ Hz}$ effective rate) and a $25$-step look-ahead offset, closes the gripper whenever any horizon step exceeds $0.6$ grasp probability, smooths poses with position EMA ($\alpha = 0.5$), quaternion SLERP, and trajectory-overlap blending ($12$), and clamps steps to a safety cage ($\le 0.08\text{ m}$, $\le 0.02\text{ rad}$) <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="App. G.3, Tab. 1" />.
  </Step>
</Stepper>

Across four real-world tasks on dual 6-DoF Trossen WidowX AI arms (40 trials per task), HumanEgo trained on $30\text{ min}$ of human video ($45$ demonstrations) achieves $92.5\%$ average success, outperforming ACT <Cite id="act-paper" /> trained on $30\text{ min}$ of robot teleoperation ($51.2\%$) by $41.3$ percentage points and beating five zero-shot human-video baselines <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§4.1, Fig. 4" />. Even with $15\text{ min}$ of human video, HumanEgo reaches $75.0\%$ average success <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§4.1, Fig. 4" />.

<Callout type="pitfall" title="Stereo hand tracking and quasi-static grasp assumptions">
HumanEgo's $95\%$ success on Serve Bread drops to $45\%$ with monocular [[mano]] trackers such as WiLoR <Cite id="wilor-paper" /> and $32.5\%$ with HaMeR because monocular RGB incurs a $5\text{–}11\text{ cm}$ depth scale ambiguity that corrupts the ICT reference frame <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§5; App. H.1, Figs. 14–15" />. In addition, because object pose is estimated once at episode start and then propagated by kinematic latching, the pipeline assumes a quasi-static scene where grasped objects do not slip or reorient inside the gripper <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§5; App. B" />.
</Callout>

## What it reuses

- [[flow-matching]]: conditional linear interpolation between $\mathbf{x}_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$ and ground-truth action chunks $\mathbf{x}_1$, integrated in $20$ Euler steps <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.4, Eq. 2; App. G.3" />.
- [[action-chunking]]: predicting a $K = 50$-step horizon per forward pass and replanning synchronously at $10\text{ Hz}$ with look-ahead offset and trajectory blending <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="§3.4; App. G.3, Tab. 1" />.
- [[transformer-block]] and [[mha]]: 6-layer `AXD` decoder with 8-head bidirectional self-attention, cross-attention over $308$ visual and ICT context tokens, and a dense GELU FFN <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="App. F.1, Tab. 1" /><Cite id="humanego-repo" where="training/FlowMatchingModel.py" />.
- [[mano]]: evaluated in the hand-tracking study via WiLoR <Cite id="wilor-paper" /> and HaMeR, showing that shared parametric bone priors give identical $1.4\text{ cm}$ Procrustes shape residuals while differing in temporal jerk <Cite id="humanego-paper" where="App. H.1, Fig. 14" />.
- Related vision-and-action systems: [[stablehand]] (which denoises world-space [[mano]] trajectories from egocentric video using per-part quality scores), [[flowhoi]] (which synthesizes coupled bimanual [[mano]] and 6D object trajectories in 3DGS scenes), and [[vla]] / [[vla-cpp]] (which train and serve vision-language-action policies with [[action-chunking]]).
