Generating dexterous hand-object interactions (HOI) for robot manipulation requires trajectories that satisfy both fine-grained finger contact on the object surface and collision-free object transport through the surrounding 3D workspace <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§I" />. Prior diffusion generators such as DiffH2O <Cite id="diffh2o-paper" /> condition only on the target object and rely on test-time gradient guidance that takes over $6\text{ s}$ per clip <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§I; Tab. I" />. FlowHOI replaces single-stage guided diffusion with a two-stage [[flow-matching]] [[dit]] that grounds bimanual [[mano]] hands and 6D object poses in a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) scene and a language instruction in $0.16\text{ s}$ <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV; Tab. I" />.

<ModelOverview model="flowhoi" />

Two structural choices define the representation and generator <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§I; §IV" />:
- **Coupled hand-object token:** each frame packs both hands' [[mano]] parameters, per-joint signed distances to the object mesh, and 6D object pose into a $117$-D token anchored to the object at the transition frame $t_g$ <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-A, Eqs. 6–10" />.
- **Two-stage $x_1$-predicted flow matching:** a grasping DiT synthesizes the $50$-frame approach prefix on a static object, and a manipulation DiT generates the full $200$-frame clip with subsequence soft inpainting for $\tau < 0.9$ <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-C, Eqs. 17–25" />.

Two further choices ground the trajectory in 3D scenes and scale training to real robots <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-B; §IV-C; §V-E" />:
- **Hybrid 3DGS and contrastive text grounding:** gated fusion of Concerto geometric and SceneSplat semantic features is compressed by a Perceiver into $256$ local cross-attention tokens alongside a voxel ViT [[adaln]] token and a TMR InfoNCE loss <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-C, Eqs. 11–16, 20–21" />.
- **EgoDex grasp curation and robot retargeting:** an automated pipeline reconstructs static-object grasp clips from hand-only EgoDex video to pretrain Stage 1, and generated clips retarget to simulated and physical bimanual Franka-Allegro robots <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-B; §V-E" />.

## Coupled hand-object representation

Predicting hand joints and object pose in independent world coordinates leaves contact implicit: small translation errors detach fingers from the object or drive them inside the mesh <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-A" />. FlowHOI instead couples both hands and the object inside every frame token $\mathbf{x}_t = (\mathbf{H}_t^l, \mathbf{H}_t^r, \mathbf{O}_t) \in \mathbb{R}^D$ ($D = 117$) and anchors hand positions to the object at the grasp-to-manipulation transition frame $t_g = N_g - 1$ <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-A, Eqs. 6–10" />.

At frame $t \in \{0, \dots, N - 1\}$, the object state $\mathbf{O}_t = (\boldsymbol{p}_t^o, \boldsymbol{r}_t^o) \in \mathbb{R}^9$ concatenates 3D translation $\boldsymbol{p}_t^o \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and continuous 6D rotation $\boldsymbol{r}_t^o \in \mathbb{R}^6$ <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-A, Eq. 7" />. Each hand $h \in \{l, r\}$ is represented by a $54$-D vector <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-A, Eq. 8" /><Cite id="flowhoi-repo" where="data_loaders/humanml/utils/data_utils.py" />:

$$
\mathbf{H}_t^h = \bigl(\tilde{\boldsymbol{p}}_t^h \in \mathbb{R}^3,\; \boldsymbol{r}_t^h \in \mathbb{R}^6,\; \boldsymbol{\theta}_t^h \in \mathbb{R}^{24},\; \mathbf{s}_t^h \in \mathbb{R}^{21}\bigr), \qquad \tilde{\boldsymbol{p}}_t^h = \boldsymbol{p}_t^h - \boldsymbol{p}_{t_g}^o
$$

Here $\boldsymbol{r}_t^h$ is the global wrist 6D orientation, $\boldsymbol{\theta}_t^h$ holds $24$ [[mano]] articulation coefficients in PCA space, and $\mathbf{s}_t^h$ records the $21$ per-joint signed distances from the hand's 3D joints $\boldsymbol{j}_k^{h,t}$ to the closest point $\Pi_{\mathcal{M}_t}(\boldsymbol{j}_k^{h,t})$ on the posed object mesh $\mathcal{M}_t$ <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-A, Eqs. 8–10" />. Anchoring $\tilde{\boldsymbol{p}}_t^h$ to $\boldsymbol{p}_{t_g}^o$ keeps the static-object grasp phase ($t \le t_g$) in an object-centric frame while allowing the manipulation phase ($t > t_g$) to transport both hands and object coherently <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-A, Eq. 10" />.

## Two-stage flow matching and soft inpainting

Training a single network to synthesize both millimeter-accurate pre-grasp closure and long-horizon 3D object transport entangles local object geometry with global scene layout <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§I; §IV-C" />. FlowHOI splits generation at the grasp completion frame $t_g = N_g - 1 = 49$: Stage 1 generates the $N_g = 50$-frame grasping prefix $\mathbf{x}^g \in \mathbb{R}^{N_g \times 108}$ on a static object, and Stage 2 generates the full $N = 200$-frame manipulation trajectory $\mathbf{x}^m \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 117}$ conditioned on the 3DGS scene <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-C, Eqs. 17–25" />.

<Stepper title="Two-stage x₁-predicted flow matching with subsequence soft inpainting">
  <Step title="Stage 1 grasp synthesis (τ: 0 → 1)">
    An MLLM extracts the grasping sub-instruction $T_g$ from the full prompt $T$. Starting from Gaussian noise $\mathbf{x}_0^g \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$, the 8-block Grasping DiT ($d = 256$) integrates 50 Euler steps conditioned on the object BPS encoding, $\mathrm{T5}(T_g)$, and the initial state $\mathbf{x}_{\text{init}}$ from frame $0$ to produce the 50-frame grasp trajectory $\mathbf{x}^g$ and transition state $\mathbf{x}_{t_g}^g$ <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-C, Eq. 17" />.
  </Step>
  <Step title="x₁-prediction along the optimal-transport path">
    Both stages use optimal-transport conditional [[flow-matching]] $\mathbf{x}_\tau = (1 - (1 - \sigma_{\min})\tau)\mathbf{x}_0 + \tau \mathbf{x}_1$ with $\sigma_{\min} = 10^{-4}$. Instead of predicting velocity $\mathbf{v}$ directly—which amplifies high-frequency jitter in 6D rotations—the DiT predicts clean motion $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 = f_\theta(\mathbf{x}_\tau, \tau, \mathbf{c})$ and converts it to velocity $\mathbf{v}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_\tau, \tau, \mathbf{c}) = (f_\theta(\mathbf{x}_\tau, \tau, \mathbf{c}) - (1 - \sigma_{\min})\mathbf{x}_\tau) / (1 - (1 - \sigma_{\min})\tau)$ <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-C, Eqs. 18–19; App. B" />.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stage 2 soft inpainting (τ < 0.9)">
    During the first 45 of 50 Euler steps ($\tau < 0.9$), the Manipulation DiT ($d = 512$) overwrites the prefix $t \le t_g$ of its noisy state $\mathbf{x}_\tau^m$ with the Stage 1 grasp trajectory $\mathbf{x}_{[0, t_g]}^g$ (padded with the static initial object pose $\mathbf{O}_0$), while hard-clamping frame $t_g$ to $\mathbf{x}_{t_g}^g$. Bidirectional self-attention propagates the grasp contact configuration into the manipulation frames $t > t_g$ <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-C, Eqs. 23–24" />.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Unmasked full-clip refinement (τ ≥ 0.9)">
    For the final 5 Euler steps ($\tau \ge 0.9$), prefix replacement is turned off—only the single transition frame $t_g$ remains clamped—so the Manipulation DiT jointly smooths velocities and contact forces across the grasp-to-manipulation boundary <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-C, Eq. 23" />.
  </Step>
</Stepper>

Both DiT backbones stack $L = 8$ identical blocks with [[adaln]] modulation, bidirectional [[mha]] temporal self-attention across all frames, cross-attention to conditioning tokens, and a GELU [[transformer-block]] feed-forward network <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-C; App. A" /><Cite id="flowhoi-repo" where="model/hoi_dit_scene.py" />.

<ModelDiagram
  model="flowhoi"
  caption="One block of the Stage 2 Manipulation DiT: AdaLN modulates each sublayer using the flow timestep τ and the ViT global scene token S_global, while cross-attention reads the T5 text tokens and 256 Perceiver local scene tokens."

Along the optimal-transport path $\mathbf{x}_\tau = (1 - (1 - \sigma_{\min})\tau)\mathbf{x}_0 + \tau \mathbf{x}_1$ with $\sigma_{\min} = 10^{-4}$, each stage trains $f_\theta$ with an $x_1$-prediction loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}} = \mathbb{E}_{\tau, \mathbf{x}_0, \mathbf{x}_1}[\|f_\theta(\mathbf{x}_\tau, \tau, \mathbf{c}) - \mathbf{x}_1\|_2^2]$ and derives the Euler ODE velocity field analytically <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§III, Eqs. 2–3; §IV-C, Eqs. 18–19" />:

$$
\mathbf{v}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_\tau, \tau, \mathbf{c}) = \frac{f_\theta(\mathbf{x}_\tau, \tau, \mathbf{c}) - (1 - \sigma_{\min})\mathbf{x}_\tau}{1 - (1 - \sigma_{\min})\tau}
$$

With binary mask $\mathbf{M}_t = 0$ for $t \le t_g$ and $\mathbf{M}_t = 1$ for $t > t_g$, Stage 2 sampling inpaints the Stage 1 prefix for $\tau < 0.9$ while clamping $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_\tau^m[t_g] \leftarrow \mathbf{x}_{t_g}^g$ at every step, and masks its training loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}}^m = \mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{M} \odot (f_\theta(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_\tau^m, \tau, \mathbf{c}^m) - \mathbf{x}_1^m)\|_2^2]$ to $t > t_g$ <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-C, Eqs. 23–25" />:

$$
\bar{\mathbf{x}}_\tau^m = \begin{cases} \mathbf{M} \odot \mathbf{x}_\tau^m + (1 - \mathbf{M}) \odot \mathbf{x}_{[0, t_g]}^g, & \tau < 0.9 \\[4pt] \mathbf{x}_\tau^m, & \tau \ge 0.9 \end{cases}
$$

Predicting clean coordinates $\mathbf{x}_1$ rather than velocity $\mathbf{v}$ directly reduces positional jerk by $113\times$ and angular jerk by $339\times$ on GRAB <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="App. B, Tab. IV" />.

## 3DGS scene and language grounding

Cross-attending a $200$-frame clip directly to thousands of 3D Gaussian primitives costs $\mathcal{O}(N \cdot N_s)$ per layer, while point geometry alone misses semantic affordances <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-C" />. FlowHOI fuses pretrained geometric and semantic features on $N_s$ Farthest-Point-Sampled Gaussian centroids $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_s \times 3}$, compresses them through a Perceiver into $L_s = 256$ local cross-attention tokens, and pairs them with a voxel ViT global token for [[adaln]] <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-C, Eqs. 11–16; App. A" />.

Frozen Concerto extracts geometric features $\mathbf{E} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_s \times d_e}$ ($d_e = 1{,}536$) and frozen SceneSplat extracts semantic features $\mathbf{U} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_s \times d_u}$ ($d_u = 768$) <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-C, Eq. 11; App. A" />. Linear maps $\Phi_e, \Phi_u$ project both streams to $d_h = 512$, where a channel-wise gate $\boldsymbol{\alpha} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_h}$ fuses them before concatenating Fourier coordinates $\gamma(\mathbf{X})$ and compressing to $L_s = 256$ queries $\mathbf{Q}_0$ via a 2-layer Perceiver <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-C, Eqs. 12–15; App. A" /><Cite id="flowhoi-hot3d-config" where="scene_perceiver_num_latents, scene_perceiver_layers" />:

$$
\mathbf{F} = \Phi_e(\mathbf{E}) + \sigma(\boldsymbol{\alpha}) \odot \Phi_u(\mathbf{U}), \qquad \mathbf{S}_{\text{local}} = \mathrm{Perceiver}\bigl(\Psi([\mathbf{F},\, \gamma(\mathbf{X})]);\, \mathbf{Q}_0\bigr) \in \mathbb{R}^{L_s \times d_s}
$$

The Perceiver cuts scene cross-attention cost from $\mathcal{O}(N \cdot N_s)$ to $\mathcal{O}(L_s \cdot N_s + N \cdot L_s)$, while a 6-layer ViT encodes a $48 \times 48 \times 24$ occupancy grid $\mathbf{V}$ into $\mathbf{S}_{\text{global}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times d_s}$ for [[adaln]] <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-C, Eqs. 15–16; App. A" /><Cite id="flowhoi-hot3d-config" where="voxel_grid_size, vit_depth" />. During training, a 4-layer Transformer motion encoder and pooled T5-Large text features are aligned with a symmetric InfoNCE loss $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}} + \lambda_{\text{align}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}$ ($\lambda_{\text{align}} = 0.1$) <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-C, Eqs. 20–21, 26; App. A" />.

## EgoDex reconstruction and robot execution

Mocap datasets with 3D object meshes (GRAB <Cite id="grab-paper" /> has $1{,}335$ clips; HOT3D provides $1{,}802$ clips) are small, whereas EgoDex provides large-scale 3D hand keypoints and text without 3D object meshes <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-B; §V-A; App. D" />. Because an object stays static before lift-off at $t_g$, FlowHOI reconstructs metric object meshes from EgoDex videos in three steps to pretrain Stage 1, and retargets Stage 2 trajectories to simulated and physical bimanual Franka-Allegro robots <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-B; §V-C; §V-E" />.

The curation pipeline smooths wrist velocity ($\sigma = 2.0$) and detects $t_g$ where speed hits a local minimum inside a $1\text{ s}$ window with a $> 30^\circ$ wrist rotation change <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-B; App. C-A" />. Across 8 frames from $[0, t_g]$, GPT-4o extracts the object noun, SAM3 segments the object, Depth Anything 3 estimates metric depth, and SAM3D reconstructs a metric mesh $\mathcal{M}$ <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-B; App. C-B" />. PyRoki IK fits [[mano]] at $t_g$ and optimizes an object offset $\Delta \boldsymbol{p}^o \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and pose correction $\Delta \boldsymbol{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^{24}$ propagated across $0 \dots t_g$ <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-B; App. C-C" />:

$$
\min_{\Delta \boldsymbol{p}^o,\, \Delta \boldsymbol{\theta}}\; \mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}} + \lambda_{\text{pen}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{pen}} + \lambda_{\text{reg}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}}
$$

Here $\mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}}$ pulls at least three fingertips (including the thumb) onto the surface while $\mathcal{L}_{\text{pen}}$ penalizes interior penetration, cutting grasp error (GE) on GRAB by $40\%$ (from $0.10\text{ mm}$ to $0.06\text{ mm}$) <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-B; §V-F, Tab. II" />. To execute on dual 7-DOF Franka Panda arms with 16-DOF, 4-finger Allegro Hand v5 hands ($\sim 1.6\times$ human scale), FlowHOI scales [[mano]] keypoints by $1.6\times$, offsets ring-finger keypoints laterally for the pinky, solves joint angles with PyRoki IK, and tracks them with a DexTrack PPO residual policy in Isaac Gym <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§V-C; §V-E; App. E; App. F" />.

## What it reuses

- [[flow-matching]]: optimal-transport linear interpolation between $\mathbf{x}_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$ and clean trajectory $\mathbf{x}_1$, integrated with a 50-step Euler ODE solver <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§III, Eqs. 2–3; §V-B" />.
- [[dit]] and [[transformer-block]]: 8-block `AXD` stack combining bidirectional [[mha]] temporal self-attention, cross-attention to text and Perceiver scene tokens, and a dense GELU FFN <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-C; App. A" />.
- [[adaln]]: adaptive layer normalization (`adaLN-Zero` in code) driven by the flow timestep $\tau$ in Stage 1 and by $\tau$ plus the ViT global scene token $\mathbf{S}_{\text{global}}$ in Stage 2 <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-C, Eq. 16; App. A" /><Cite id="flowhoi-repo" where="model/hoi_dit_scene.py" />.
- [[mano]]: parametric 3D hand surface model providing the $24$-D PCA articulation space and $21$ 3D joints for both hands <Cite id="flowhoi-paper" where="§IV-A, Eq. 8" />.
- Related motion and action systems: [[stablehand]] (which uses the same 8-block `AXD` flow-matching DiT backbone for observation-anchored hand motion recovery) and [[vla]] (which maps visual and language context directly to robot actions).
