DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (September 2026) rebuilds [[deepseek-v4]]'s Flash model around a cheaper million-token path: a causal encoder-decoder split, cross-layer sharing of the sparse-attention state, hashed n-gram memory, an FP4 KV cache, and native image input. It trains on 45T multimodal tokens and replaces [[mtp]] with a separately trained drafting module <Cite id="deepseek-v4-1-flash-report" where="Abstract, §2.1" />.

## The whole model

Forty layers in two halves: a causal encoder that runs once per prompt, and a decoder whose global keys and values are projected from the encoder's final states <Cite id="deepseek-v4-1-flash-report" where="§2.2" />. Every block is a pre-norm [[transformer-block]] with sparse attention and a MoE feed-forward.

## What is new

### A causal encoder-decoder

V4 spent the same layer stack on prompt and generation. V4.1-Flash splits the work in two, in the spirit of YOCO <Cite id="deepseek-v4-1-flash-report" where="§2.2" />: a 20-layer causal encoder reads the prompt once, and the decoder's global keys and values come from the encoder's final hidden states rather than from each decoder layer <Cite id="deepseek-v4-1-flash-report" where="§2.2, §4.2.1" />. The report's claim is that this nearly halves prefill computation <Cite id="deepseek-v4-1-flash-report" where="§2.1" />. Generation is unchanged: one token per step, reading the projected global state plus the decoder's own sparse attention.

### Cross-layer sharing and a hierarchical indexer

CSA2, the second generation of V4's sparse attention, lets layers share their work <Cite id="deepseek-v4-1-flash-report" where="§2.3" />. A **Full** layer computes its global keys and values and its indexer keys, and writes them for its group. A **Reindex** layer runs its own indexer over the shared keys. A **Reuse** layer reuses both the shared state and the top-k indices already computed <Cite id="deepseek-v4-1-flash-report" where="§2.3" />.

The indexer itself becomes hierarchical: unlike [[dsa|V3.2's indexer]], which scored individual positions, it first picks blocks of positions, then positions inside those blocks — up to 16,384 candidates before the final top-k is taken <Cite id="deepseek-v4-1-flash-config" where="text_config.candidate_*" />. The encoder compresses its sequence by 2, the decoder by 1 <Cite id="deepseek-v4-1-flash-report" where="§4.2.1" />.

Attention only looks back through keys and values computed for the current context. [[engram]] adds a second route: two modules, at layers 1 and 14, keep hashed tables of which short token runs followed which during training, and every token can look its own n-gram up <Cite id="deepseek-v4-1-flash-report" where="§2.4.2" />. The tables are hashed rather than embedded — no vocabulary-sized matrix, just fixed-width hash heads storing about 16M entries each in FP8 — 196B parameters of memory in two modules <Cite id="deepseek-v4-1-flash-report" where="§2.4.2; §4.2.2" />. The mechanism, including the vocabulary projection and the context-aware gate, is in [[engram]].

### DSpark replaces MTP

Where [[deepseek-v4]] kept [[mtp]] inside pre-training, V4.1-Flash omits it and trains DSpark afterwards, with the backbone frozen <Cite id="deepseek-v4-1-flash-report" where="§2.1; §2.4.3" />. DSpark is three transformer blocks with a sliding window and a low-rank Markov head; it drafts five positions per pass, and the main model verifies them on a schedule driven by the drafter's confidence <Cite id="deepseek-v4-1-flash-report" where="§2.4.3" />.

<Callout type="note">
The config still sets `num_nextn_predict_layers` — to 3. The report omits MTP entirely; the value matches the three DSpark drafter blocks, so the field appears to be reused for DSpark rather than describing a prediction depth <Cite id="deepseek-v4-1-flash-config" where="text_config.num_nextn_predict_layers" />.
</Callout>

### An FP4 KV cache

The main cache is stored in 4-bit floating point: E2M1 values with one FP8 scale per 16 channels — the shape of [[nvfp4]] without its global scale <Cite id="deepseek-v4-1-flash-report" where="§2.4.4" />. A decode step over a full context holds 890 bytes per token, about a quarter of V4-Flash's cache, and the cache kept between turns is about an eighth of V4-Flash's, because only the most recent window tokens need replay <Cite id="deepseek-v4-1-flash-report" where="Abstract; §3.2.2" />. Growing the context from 4K to 1M raises decode FLOPs by only a quarter <Cite id="deepseek-v4-1-flash-report" where="§1" />.

V4.1-Flash reads images natively: the DeepSeek-ViT tower feeds a two-layer MLP projector into the language hidden size, and image tokens get their own load balancing beside the text tokens <Cite id="deepseek-v4-1-flash-report" where="§2.1.1; §4.2.1" />. The vision side lives in the config's `vision_config`; this note follows the language model.

## What it reuses

- [[dsa]] — the indexer-and-selection attention, now in its second generation, CSA2 <Cite id="deepseek-v4-1-flash-report" where="§2.3" />.
- [[deepseek-moe]] — top-6 of 384 routed experts plus a shared one, in every block of both halves <Cite id="deepseek-v4-1-flash-config" where="text_config.n_routed_experts, num_experts_per_tok" />.
- [[aux-loss-free-balancing]] — bias-based balancing, now applied separately to image and text tokens <Cite id="deepseek-v4-1-flash-report" where="§4.2.2" />.
- The mHC residual from [[deepseek-v4]] — kept as Single-Pass mHC, with a fused kernel that halves activation-memory traffic <Cite id="deepseek-v4-1-flash-report" where="§2.4.1" />.
- [[fp8-training]] — FP8 weights, now in 32×32 blocks, with FP4 experts <Cite id="deepseek-v4-1-flash-config" where="text_config.quantization_config" />.
- [[rope]] — partial RoPE plus YaRN, extended to a million tokens.
