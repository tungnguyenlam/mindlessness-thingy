Nemotron 3 Super "scales up the hybrid Mamba-Attention MoE architecture introduced in Nemotron-3 Nano" <Cite id="nemotron-3-super-report" where="§2.1" />. The layer recipe is the one described in [[hybrid-ssm-attention]]: mostly Mamba-2 layers, a few attention layers, and MoE feed-forward layers between them. It is also the first Nemotron model with three additions: [[latent-moe]], [[mtp]] and pretraining in [[nvfp4]] <Cite id="nemotron-3-super-report" where="Abstract, §1" />. This note covers those three and the scale-up. Follow the links for everything else.

## What changed from Nemotron 3 Nano

### Bigger in every direction

[[nemotron-3-nano]] has 52 layers, hidden size 2688, and 6 active of 128 routed experts <Cite id="nemotron-3-nano-report" where="Table 1" />. Super grows that to 88 layers, hidden size 4096, and 22 active of 512 <Cite id="nemotron-3-super-report" where="Table 1" />. Both keep the same attention shape: 32 query heads sharing 2 key/value heads.

### LatentMoE

The routed experts work in a 1024-wide latent instead of the 4096-wide hidden state, a compression of 4. That cuts the weight bytes loaded per expert and the bytes sent between GPUs per token, and the savings pay for more experts per token. See [[latent-moe]]. The router and the single shared expert still see the full hidden state <Cite id="nemotron-3-super-report" where="§2.1.1" />.

### Multi-token prediction with shared weights

Like [[deepseek-v3]], Super trains with [[mtp]]. Here the MTP module's weights are shared across its prediction steps, so at inference one head can be applied recursively to draft several tokens <Cite id="nemotron-3-super-report" where="§2.1.2" />. At draft length 7 the average acceptance length on SPEED-Bench is 3.45 <Cite id="nemotron-3-super-report" where="Table 2" />.

### Pretraining in 4 bits

Super is pretrained in [[nvfp4]]. Elements are E2M1 floats in 16-element blocks, each block has an E4M3 scale, and each tensor has an FP32 scale. Some parts stay in higher precision: the final 15% of the network, the latent projections, the MTP layers, the QKV and attention parts in BF16, and the Mamba output projection in MXFP8 <Cite id="nemotron-3-super-report" where="§2.2, Table 3" />. Nano 3 was trained in BF16 <Cite id="nemotron-3-super-report" where="§2.2, Fig. 7" />.

## Shared with the rest of the family

- **Expert balancing.** A sigmoid router with a per-expert bias ([[aux-loss-free-balancing]], update rate $10^{-3}$) plus a small standard balancing loss ($10^{-4}$). This is the same recipe as DeepSeek-V3 <Cite id="nemotron-3-super-report" where="§2.4" />.
- **Squared-ReLU experts.** `mlp_hidden_act: relu2` <Cite id="nemotron-3-super-config" where="mlp_hidden_act" />, the activation Primer found most effective for transformer feed-forward blocks <Cite id="primer-paper" where="§3" />.
- **Plain plumbing.** No positional embeddings, no dropout, no biases, RMSNorm, and untied input/output embeddings <Cite id="nemotron-3-super-report" where="§2.1.3" />.

<Callout type="pitfall">
The config contains `rope_theta: 10000`, but the report states that positional embeddings are omitted "consistent with prior Nemotron models" <Cite id="nemotron-3-super-report" where="§2.1.3" />. The key looks like an unused default of the config class. Don't read it as evidence of RoPE. Whether the modeling code reads it is unverified.
</Callout>

Pretraining ran for 25T tokens, with the learning rate decaying over the last 5T. A long-context stage at 1,048,576 tokens followed <Cite id="nemotron-3-super-report" where="§2.4, §2.6" />.

## What came next

[[nemotron-3-5-super-vl]] starts from this model's base checkpoint, adds a vision encoder, and reworks the MTP head and the training pipeline. Nemotron 3 Ultra applies the same architecture at 550B total and 55B active parameters <Cite id="nemotron-3-ultra-report" where="Abstract, §2.1" />.
