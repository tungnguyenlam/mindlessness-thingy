LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct is the general-purpose chat checkpoint in the 1.2B LFM2.5 family. The stack is the same [[hybrid-conv-attention]] interleaving as [[lfm2-5-350m]] — 10 [[gated-short-conv]] layers and 6 [[gqa]] layers — at twice the hidden width <Cite id="lfm2-5-1-2b-instruct-card" where="Number of layers" />. Pretraining is 28T tokens, up from the 10T LFM2 budget, then SFT, preference alignment, and multi-stage RL <Cite id="lfm2-5-intro-blog" />. [[lfm2-5-1-2b-thinking]] is this backbone with a reasoning post-train.

<Callout type="pitfall">
The LFM2 report lists feed-forward width 8192 for LFM2-1.2B <Cite id="lfm2-report" where="Table 1" />. This checkpoint's config has `intermediate_size: 12288`. Use the config. `max_position_embeddings` is 128000; the card's supported context is 32,768.
</Callout>

## What is new

### Wider 16-layer hybrid, 28T, then instruct post-train

Hidden size doubles from the 350M's 1024 to 2048, query heads from 16 to 32, KV heads stay 8, so the [[gqa]] group size is 4 rather than 2 <Cite id="lfm2-5-1-2b-instruct-config" where="num_attention_heads" />. `layer_types` matches the 350M interleaving exactly <Cite id="lfm2-5-1-2b-instruct-config" where="layer_types" />.

The January 2026 family announcement describes Instruct as the default chat variant, trained with supervised fine-tuning, preference alignment, and large-scale multi-stage reinforcement learning <Cite id="lfm2-5-intro-blog" />. The card recommends it for agentic tasks, extraction and RAG, and not for knowledge-heavy work or programming <Cite id="lfm2-5-1-2b-instruct-card" />.

A 296M speculative-decoding drafter (LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct-DSpark) is released alongside; the card quotes about 2.1× faster decode in SGLang with identical outputs <Cite id="lfm2-5-1-2b-instruct-card" />.

## What it reuses

- [[hybrid-conv-attention]] with kernel-3 [[gated-short-conv]] and 6 [[gqa]] layers, the same layout as [[lfm2-5-350m]].
- [[rope]] on attention layers (`rope_theta` 1,000,000) and a 65,536-token vocabulary.
- [[kv-cache]] only in the 6 attention layers.
