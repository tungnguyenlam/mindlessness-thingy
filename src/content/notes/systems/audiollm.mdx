Most speech-enabled language models bolt a 300M to 600M pretrained acoustic encoder onto an LLM, making the front-end larger than a compact edge backbone. **AudioLLM** drops the external speech encoder altogether and teaches [[lfm2-5-230m|LFM2.5-230M-Base]] to read 80-bin HTK log-mel spectrograms through a 6.95M-parameter strided convolutional projector and 15.53M LoRA weights <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="README.md, audiollm/model.py" />. Reaching $11.87\%$ word error rate (WER) on LibriSpeech `test-clean` required solving four alignment bottlenecks: embedding norm mismatch, LoRA gradient starvation, acoustic frame redundancy inside the [[hybrid-conv-attention]] stack, and [[byte-level-bpe]] fertility explosion on non-English scripts <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="WORKLOG.md" />.

<ModelOverview model="audiollm" />

<Words>

The pipeline converts a 16 kHz mono waveform into 100 Hz log-mel frames, downsamples them $8\times$ into 12.5 Hz embeddings of width $d = 1{,}024$, and prepends them to the text prompt `"Transcription:"` inside the frozen [[lfm2-5-230m]] backbone <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="audiollm/features.py, audiollm/model.py" />. Only the convolutional projector ($6.95\text{M}$ parameters) and rank-$64$ LoRA adapters ($15.53\text{M}$ parameters across all linear projections) are updated during training, leaving $22.49\text{M}$ trainable parameters out of $252.56\text{M}$ total ($8.9\%$) <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="audiollm/backbone.py, WORKLOG.md" />.

During training, cross-entropy loss is evaluated only on the transcript and end-of-sequence tokens (`labels = -100` on the prefix), and the vocabulary head `lm_head` is sliced to run exclusively on target positions so the $[B, T_a, V]$ audio logit tensor is never materialized <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="audiollm/model.py" />.

</Words>

## Strided log-mel projection and scale-matching initialization

Feeding raw spectrograms into a decoder-only LLM fails when consecutive frames are flattened into a single linear layer (`STACK = 16`, 160 ms per token) or initialized with standard PyTorch weights <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="docs/worklog_archive/2026-10.md" />. Following Mel-LLM <Cite id="mel-llm-paper" where="§2" />, AudioLLM replaces flat frame stacking with $S = \log_2(K)$ stages of strided temporal convolution followed by a two-layer MLP and embedding-scale initialization (`match_emb`) <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="audiollm/projectors/conv.py, audiollm/projectors/base.py" />.

<Words>

The acoustic front-end computes a 400-point short-time Fourier transform (25 ms Hann window, 10 ms hop, 201 bins) and projects magnitudes onto $F_m = 80$ triangular HTK mel filters with log compression $\log(\text{mel} + 10^{-6})$ and global per-bin mean-variance normalization (MVN) estimated over $2{,}000$ training utterances <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="audiollm/config.py, audiollm/features.py" />. At downsampling factor $K = 8$ (12.5 Hz, 80 ms stride), `ConvAudioProjector` runs three stride-2, kernel-3 convolutional stages ($80 \to 512 \to 1{,}024 \to 1{,}024$, GELU) whose receptive field spans $2K - 1 = 15$ mel frames (150 ms), overlapping adjacent tokens by 70 ms so phoneme transitions are never split across hard block boundaries <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="audiollm/projectors/conv.py" />.

To prevent AMD ROCm MIOpen from stalling for over 8 seconds per step when compiling dynamic-length 1D convolutions, each strided convolution is implemented via `F.pad` + tensor `unfold` followed by `nn.Linear(3 * C_in, C_out)`, routing every layer through hipBLAS matrix multiplication <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="audiollm/projectors/conv.py, WORKLOG.md" />.

Standard Kaiming initialization on unit-variance MVN inputs produces audio vectors with per-dimension standard deviation $0.58$ and row norm $\|a\|_2 \approx 18.5$, which is $20\times$ larger than [[lfm2-5-230m]] token embeddings ($\sigma_{\text{emb}} = 0.028$, $\|e\|_2 \approx 0.89$) <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="docs/worklog_archive/2026-10.md" />. The `match_emb` initializer sets the projector's output matrix to:

$$W_{\text{out}} \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\; \frac{\sigma_{\text{emb}}^2}{d_{\text{in}}}\right), \qquad b_{\text{out}} = 0$$

This brings step-0 audio tokens to $\|a\|_2 \approx 0.83$, matching the native residual stream scale of [[layer-norm|RMSNorm]] inside the frozen backbone <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="audiollm/projectors/base.py" />.

</Words>

## Breaking the 120% WER text-prior regime

Even with scale-matched embeddings, early linear-projector runs plateaued at validation loss $3.45\text{–}3.61$ and $112\text{–}125\%$ WER, producing identical error rates when audio tokens were randomly shuffled across time (`wer == wer_shuffle`) <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="docs/worklog_archive/2026-10.md" />. The backbone ignored the acoustic prefix and functioned as an unconditional text language model, while unguarded greedy decoding occasionally fell into repetition loops that inflated single-utterance WER past $2{,}000\%$ <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="docs/worklog_archive/2026-10.md" />.

<Words>

Per-component gradient telemetry in `audiollm/trainer.py` exposed why shared gradient clipping stalled learning: early in training, LoRA parameter gradients averaged $\|\nabla_{\text{lora}}\|_2 \approx 2.2$ while projector gradients averaged $\|\nabla_{\text{proj}}\|_2 \approx 0.18$ <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="docs/worklog_archive/2026-10.md, audiollm/trainer.py" />. Clipping the combined parameter vector to norm $1.0$ scaled the projector step down by more than $10\times$, allowing LoRA to rapidly fit the corpus text distribution before the projector moved.

Clipping $\|\nabla_{\text{proj}}\|_2 \le 1.0$ and $\|\nabla_{\text{lora}}\|_2 \le 1.0$ separately (with learning rates $\eta_{\text{proj}} = 10^{-3}$ and $\eta_{\text{lora}} = 3 \times 10^{-4}$), switching to `ConvAudioProjector` at `STACK = 8`, and training beyond $3{,}000$ steps triggered a sharp phase transition where $\|\nabla_{\text{proj}}\|_2$ jumped $5\times$ (from $0.26$ to $1.3\text{–}2.3$) and validation loss dropped from $3.35$ to $2.03$ <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="docs/worklog_archive/2026-10.md" />. At inference, capping generated tokens at $\min(256, \lceil 6.0 \cdot \text{sec} \rceil + 10)$ and blocking repeated 4-grams (`no_repeat_ngram_size = 4`) cut 9-set mean WER on the initial 544-hour checkpoint from $121.7\%$ to $95.3\%$ without retraining <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="audiollm/model.py, docs/worklog_archive/2026-10.md" />.

</Words>

## Implicit acoustic encoding and layer-2 affinity token merging

In encoder-free multimodal models, early decoder blocks act as an implicit modality encoder whose adjacent token representations grow progressively smoother with depth <Cite id="implicit-encoders-paper" where="§3" />. Probing mean-centered adjacent audio cosine similarity $\cos(\tilde{h}_i, \tilde{h}_{i+1})$ across all 14 layers of [[lfm2-5-230m]] confirms this inside a [[hybrid-conv-attention]] backbone: similarity climbs from $0.285$ at the projector output to $0.354$ after the first two [[gated-short-conv]] blocks (layers 0 and 1) and reaches $0.456\text{–}0.525$ in layers $9\text{–}13$ on `dev-clean` <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="WORKLOG.md, scripts/probe_layer_redundancy.py" />.

<Words>

Because layers 0 and 1 are [[gated-short-conv]] blocks with $O(T)$ compute and a 3-token state, whereas all six [[gqa]] layers sit at indices $k \in \{2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12\}$, applying training-free Affinity Pooling <Cite id="affinity-pooling-paper" where="§3" /> at layer $k = 2$ lets the two initial conv blocks smooth raw 12.5 Hz frames before **100% of the attention layers** run <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="audiollm/layer_merging.py, WORKLOG.md" />. At layer $k$, greedy left-to-right pooling merges up to $M_{\max}$ consecutive audio hidden states whenever $\cos(h_i, h_{i+1}) \ge \tau$, while leaving prompt and transcript tokens untouched <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="audiollm/merging.py" />.

To compare merging at different depths $k \in \{0, \dots, 13\}$ under a single compute budget, AudioLLM defines the effective token ratio $E$ as the average fraction of audio tokens processed per layer across all $L = 14$ layers <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="docs/in_llm_merging_plan.md" />:

$$E = \frac{k + (L - k) R_k}{L} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad R_k = \frac{L \cdot E - k}{L - k}$$

Here $R_k = T'_a / T_a$ is the retained audio token fraction after merging at layer $k$.

</Words>

## Tokenizer fertility, cross-backbone transfer, and LFM3 grafting

Applying AudioLLM to Vietnamese speech exposed a severe bottleneck in [[lfm2-tokenizer]]: because its 65,536-entry [[byte-level-bpe]] table was trained without Vietnamese merges, precomposed UTF-8 diacritics fragment into raw byte tokens at **$3.23$ tokens per word** ($221\%$ fertility overhead), requiring ~40 BPE tokens and $3.2\times$ more autoregressive decoding steps for a 4-second utterance <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="WORKLOG.md, docs/idea_lfm3_tokenizer_graft.md" />.

<Words>

AudioLLM tests two remedies for high tokenizer fertility <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="WORKLOG.md" />:

1. **Cross-backbone projector transfer (`INIT_PROJ_FROM`)**: Because `Qwen3-0.6B-Base` shares hidden size $d = 1{,}024$ with [[lfm2-5-230m]] and tokenizes Vietnamese at $1.17$ tokens/word, the English `E2` `ConvAudioProjector` transfers directly into Qwen3 by rescaling its final linear layer to Qwen3's embedding spread ($\sigma_{\text{emb}} = 0.0256$) <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="WORKLOG.md" />. On a 500-hour Vietnamese mixture (`223,115` utterances), Qwen3-0.6B reached $35.48\%$ WER on GigaSpeech2-vi ($11.64\%$ on PhoAudiobook, $11.88\%$ on LSVSC), beating the un-grafted LFM2.5-230M baseline by $12.89$ points <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="WORKLOG.md" />.
2. **Merge-closed `LFM3` tokenizer grafting**: To keep the fast 230M [[hybrid-conv-attention]] backbone, `scripts/graft_lfm3_tokenizer.py` replaces [[lfm2-tokenizer]] with `LFM3`'s multilingual tokenizer pruned to a merge-closed English+Vietnamese vocabulary of $45{,}755$ tokens <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="WORKLOG.md, docs/idea_lfm3_tokenizer_graft.md" />. The $35{,}278$ shared tokens keep their exact `LFM2.5-230M` embeddings, while the $10{,}477$ new Vietnamese tokens are initialized as the mean of their constituent `LFM2.5` sub-token vectors and warmed up for $1{,}500$ steps before unfreezing the backbone <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="WORKLOG.md" />. Combining this graft ($1.14$ tokens/word) with prompt-triggered ASR error correction (`CORRECTION_P = 0.2`, training the same model to both transcribe `[BOS][audio]["Transcription:"]` and repair tone/homophone errors via `[BOS][audio]["Draft: ... Correction:"]`) reached $16.33\%$ WER on the 300-utterance ZAC Vietnamese dev set at $6{,}000$ steps <Cite id="audiollm-repo" where="WORKLOG.md, audiollm/vietnamese.py" />.

</Words>
