A [[moe]] layer grows parameters without growing compute per token, but the standard recipe — a few large experts and a top-$k$ router — spreads knowledge badly across experts. The DeepSeekMoE paper names two symptoms: knowledge hybridity, where an expert that receives many kinds of token stores unrelated things, and knowledge redundancy, where separate experts separately learn the same common facts <Cite id="deepseekmoe-paper" where="§1" />. Two changes address the two symptoms.

The routed lane is the [[moe]] mechanism with a finer cut. The shared lane is new: its weights are read by every token, so the router only ever splits the rest of the budget.

## Fine-grained experts

Each expert is a full feed-forward network. DeepSeekMoE cuts each one into $m$ smaller experts by shrinking the intermediate width to $1/m$ of the original, and activates $m$ times as many of them. Total expert parameters stay fixed, and so does the work per token: the layer now holds $mN$ experts where it held $N$, with $mK$ non-zero gates per token instead of $K$ <Cite id="deepseekmoe-paper" where="§3.1" />.

What changes is the choice. With $N = 16$ experts and top-2 routing a token reaches one of 120 combinations. Split each expert into 4 and the same parameter and compute budget offers $\binom{64}{8}$ combinations <Cite id="deepseekmoe-paper" where="§3.1" />. A token can assemble the exact mix it needs instead of picking from a handful of coarse blocks, and at fixed parameters and fixed compute the paper's ablation improves steadily as the grain gets finer <Cite id="deepseekmoe-paper" where="§4.4" />.

## Shared experts

A finer grain alone still leaves every routed expert relearning the facts that tokens of all kinds need. DeepSeekMoE sets $K_s$ experts aside as shared ones: the router is not consulted and every token runs them, while the routed lane gives up $K_s$ activations to keep the compute budget constant <Cite id="deepseekmoe-paper" where="§3.2" />.

$$
h_t = \sum_{i=1}^{K_s} \mathrm{FFN}_i(u_t) + \sum_{i=K_s+1}^{mN} g_{i,t} \mathrm{FFN}_i(u_t)
$$

The shared lane absorbs the common knowledge so the routed experts can specialise in what differs. The paper measures the cost of dropping it: swap the shared expert for one more routed expert at the same compute and the Pile loss rises from 1.808 to 2.414 <Cite id="deepseekmoe-paper" where="§4.5" />.

<Callout type="pitfall">
Fine-grained does not mean bigger. $m$ experts at $1/m$ the width hold exactly the parameters one full-width expert held; the gain comes from how many of them a token can activate together, not from a larger layer.
</Callout>

## What a model chose

[[deepseek-v2]] is the first model to ship the design, at a scale far above the paper's validation models: 160 routed experts of which each token reaches 6, plus 2 shared, with expert intermediate width 1536 <Cite id="deepseek-v2-config" where="config.json" />.
