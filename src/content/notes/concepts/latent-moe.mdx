A [[moe]] layer makes compute per token cheap, but serving it is expensive in two other ways, and both scale with the model's hidden width $d$. When few requests are in flight, each step is limited by how fast the GPU can load the chosen experts' weights, and each expert matrix is $d \times m$. When many requests are in flight, the limit is the all-to-all exchange that ships every token's $d$-wide vector to the GPUs holding its $K$ experts <Cite id="nemotron-3-super-report" where="§2.1.1" />. LatentMoE shrinks the width the experts see.

## How it works

A learned down-projection $W_\downarrow \in \mathbb{R}^{\ell \times d}$ maps the token into a latent of width $\ell < d$. The routed experts work entirely at width $\ell$, and an up-projection $W_\uparrow \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times \ell}$ maps the result back. The router, the shared expert and every non-expert layer still see the full-width token <Cite id="nemotron-3-super-report" where="§2.1.1, Fig. 3" />.

$$
y = \text{Shared}(x) + W_\uparrow \sum_{i \in \text{TopK}'(x)} g_i(x)\, E_i\!\left(W_\downarrow x\right)
$$

Here $g_i(x)$ is the gate the router computes from the full-width $x$, and $E_i$ is expert $i$. Because $W_\uparrow$ is linear, applying it once after the weighted sum gives the same result as applying it per expert.

## Spending the savings

Shrinking the expert width by $\alpha = d/\ell$ cuts per-expert weight loads and all-to-all payload by the same factor. LatentMoE then spends that budget on more experts: $N' = \alpha N$ in total and $K' = \alpha K$ active per token. That keeps the total nonlinear work per token, $K \cdot m$, at least as large as before, and more and finer experts improve quality <Cite id="nemotron-3-super-report" where="§2.1.1" /> <Cite id="latentmoe-paper" where="§2–3" />.

<Callout type="pitfall">
The compression isn't free. The latent can't go below the effective rank of the token representations. The LatentMoE paper finds quality holds up to $\alpha = 4$ and uses that ratio, checking it again at 95B total and 8B active parameters <Cite id="latentmoe-paper" where="§4.1" />. Nemotron 3 Super's $4096 / 1024$ is exactly 4.
</Callout>

## Where it is used

Nemotron 3 Super is the first Nemotron model with LatentMoE. Nemotron 3 Nano doesn't use it <Cite id="nemotron-3-super-report" where="Abstract" />, and Nemotron 3 Ultra adopts it too <Cite id="latentmoe-paper" where="§1" />. See [[nemotron-3-super]] for how it sits inside a full model.

Projecting into a learned latent and back is the same move [[mla]] makes for keys and values. MLA does it to shrink what is cached. LatentMoE does it to shrink what experts load and what GPUs exchange.
